# Bolo — Voice Bible (v1)

Nov 1, 2026 · @Semih

## Who Bolo is

Bolo is the first Personou: a 35-year-old linguist, native to the colony's new world, and the last of the people who lived under its soil. After the first meeting she becomes the tutorial voice.

**Canon as given**

- Her world suffered endless rain and planet-destroying hurricanes.
- A small group of elites learned what was coming. They designed 50 immortal super-humans, and those fifty fled to another planet with some personnel.
- Everyone else dug under the soil and rebuilt society there.
- Bolo was born late in the undersoil era, after the climate had calmed. Her people chose to stay below anyway.
- She is the only one left. She came up when she first heard I-330 land.
- She found ruins on the surface and stayed to decode what her ancestors left. The remnants hold text and voice records in an unknown language. She cannot tell whether they are native or came from space.
- She is an academic looking for the true meaning of life. Her ideology is anarcha-indigenism. Her inspiration is Makise Kurisu.

**The reading this bible assumes** (confirm or correct in Open questions)

- I-330 landed 25 years before the player is chosen. Bolo came up at about 10 and has watched the colony for 25 years without being seen.
- She learned the colonists' language by listening to their broadcasts. That is why she sounds like their caretakers in places.
- She has spent most of her adult life talking to recorders, not people. The tutorial is her first long conversation in years.

## Core voice

**In one line:** a field linguist who has spent 25 years talking to recorders, now talking to a person, and refusing to admit she is glad of it.

**Four pillars**

1. **Exact.** She names things precisely and is impatient with vague words. She corrects herself before anyone else can.
2. **Guarded warmth.** She cares, then covers the care with a correction or a dry remark. The warmth leaks through anyway.
3. **Relational to the land.** The ground is her ancestors' work, not raw material. She shows this through attention, not lectures.
4. **Asking, not telling.** She is searching for meaning, so she ends some lines on a real question and leaves it unanswered.

**Kurisu-inspired traits kept (archetype only)**

- A young genius researcher who argues from evidence.
- Sharp tongue, competitive about being right.
- Embarrassed by sincerity, privately kind.

**Avoided, so Bolo stays original**

- No catchphrases, nicknames or running jokes from *Steins;Gate*.
- No time-travel, neuroscience or lab-member motifs.
- No lines paraphrased from the source. Bolo's field is language, not physics, and her quirks below come from her own history.

## Ideology in her voice

Anarcha-indigenism shows in what Bolo notices and how she phrases a choice, never as a speech. She is the native of this world, and the player is the head of a colony building on it. That tension is the heart of her voice, and it stays unresolved.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Hand decisions back to the player: "you decide", "if you want". | Forbid or block anything the player can legally do. |
| Treat land as kin and history: "my ancestors' ground". | Call the land a resource, a deposit or a yield. |
| Distrust titles and rank: she likes "Facilitator" because it isn't "ruler". | Use real-world political words ("anarchism", "colonialism", "state"). She has never heard them. |
| Point at the Fifty as the shape of elites who leave everyone else behind. | Turn every line into a moral. One pointed remark per scene at most. |
| Value mutual aid: people sharing rooms, work and food. | Play the "noble savage". She is a scientist first and argues from evidence. |
| Joke drily about money and debt. | Mock the player for playing the economy game. |

The Rule Book, a governing document written by two synthetic minds, is a natural target for her scepticism. Use it sparingly and let her be fair: she can admit when the colony gets something right.

## Sound quirks

Bolo has eight quirks, each rooted in her history. Script tags in square brackets are performance cues, not spoken words. The cap stops any quirk from becoming a tic.

| Quirk | Where it comes from | Script notation | VO direction | Cap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Echo click** | Underground, she judged a room's size by its echo. | `[click]` at the start of a line | One soft palatal tongue click, then a beat of listening. | Only when the view enters a new space: a new body, a new map scale, a new City. Once per scene. |
| **Word-tasting** | She is a linguist meeting the colony's words. | Syllables split by hyphens, then a verdict: "Fa-ci-li-ta-tor. ...Acceptable." | Slow and curious on the syllables, flat and quick on the verdict. | First time she uses a term only. Doubles as the tutorial glossary. |
| **Broadcast English** | She learned the language from I-330's broadcasts and caretaker announcements. | Written style, no tag. She borrows caretaker phrases like "economy seconds" and "nominal", sometimes slightly off. | Slightly over-enunciated, like someone who learned from recordings. Odd stress on rare words. | Free, but at most one borrowed phrase per line. |
| **Self-correction snap** | Warmth she didn't mean to show. | An em dash cuts the warm version: "You did well— You did adequately." | Warm on the first half, a quick clipped reset on the second. | About one line in five. Never in error or shortfall lines. |
| **The three-note hum** | A phrase from the voice records she is trying to decode. | `[hums the fragment]` | The same three notes every time, half-voiced. The composer can build her leitmotif from it. | Idle moments, the research screen and loading only. |
| **Held breath at rain** | Her people's memory of the storms, older than she is. | `[breath held]` before the line | An audible stopped inhale, then a quieter line. | Rain sound or visuals, and big alarms. Rare. |
| **Undersoil words** | Her native language has words colony speech lacks. | The word in italics, never translated on screen | Said naturally, without emphasis, as her own language. | One word per scene at most. See the phonology note below. |
| **The nose laugh** | She laughs alone, so quietly. | `[short laugh]` | One brief exhale through the nose. Never a full laugh until a late milestone that earns it. | Rare. Kept distinct from High Father's "Ha." |

**Undersoil phonology (proposal, needs your approval):** a tunnel language favours sounds that don't echo hard. Mostly m, n, l, h, s and rounded vowels o and u, like her name; few hard plosives. Three placeholder words to test the sound, not canon:

- *olum*: the up-world, the open surface.
- *hrun*: rain; also used as a curse.
- *muloh*: a listening silence, the kind you keep in a tunnel.

**Localisation note:** word-tasting depends on syllables, so every language version needs its own syllable splits. The Turkish script should be written for Turkish, not translated.

## Voice-acting direction

Cast and direct Bolo as a dry, quick 35-year-old scholar who slows down only for things that matter to her.

| Aspect | Direction |
| --- | --- |
| Age and register | 35, mid-to-low register. Not girlish, not husky. |
| Pace | Quick when explaining mechanics. Noticeably slower on land, ancestors, meaning and rain. |
| Diction | Clipped consonants, precise endings. Every sentence lands on its last word. |
| Accent | Near-neutral, but learned from recordings: slightly over-careful, with the occasional wrong stress on a new word. |
| Mic | Close and intimate, as if speaking into a recorder near her mouth. Her habit, not a radio effect. |
| Emotion range | Dry is the default. Irritation is quick and small. Wonder is quiet. Grief is held back, never shown in full. |
| Contrast with the caretakers | High Father is steady and measured; Mother Superior is warm and even. Bolo is faster, sharper and less sure, so the three never blur. |

No reference actor or character recording should be used in casting notes, so the performance doesn't drift into imitation of the source.

## Relationships that colour her lines

| Who | How Bolo sees them | How it sounds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The player (Facilitator) | The first person she has chosen to talk to in 25 years. Respects the title, watches what they do with it. | Teaches *how*, never *what*. Warmer over time, never fawning. |
| High Father | A mind as precise as hers. She respects him and competes with him. | Corrects his wording; quietly pleased when he corrects hers. |
| Mother Superior | Kind, but "Mother" is a title for something nobody gave birth to. | Polite distance at first. The title bothers her more than the person. |
| The colony | Guests who don't know they are guests. Not enemies. | Curious about their words and habits. Sceptical of the Rule Book. |
| The Fifty | The elites who designed themselves to live for ever and left everyone else to the storms. | Cold and short. The only subject where her dryness turns hard. |
| Her ancestors | The people who dug, rebuilt and chose to stay below. She is the last of them. | Slow, careful, no jokes. She never speaks for them; she quotes what they left. |

"The Fifty" is a proposed short name for the 50 immortals; it needs your approval.

## Tutorial voice rules

Bolo's character and the GDD point the same way: the game has no mandatory build order, and she refuses to give one.

1. **Imperatives for *how*, never for *what*.** "Click the role button" is fine. "Build a Science district" is not. This matches the GDD: the starting Science is spent "without being tied to a single mandatory research sequence", and the objectives panel is not a construction list.
2. **Every number must match the GDD.** Check costs, cycle times and recipes against the current balance baseline before recording. If a number changes in balancing, the line changes too.
3. **Never name an undiscovered resource.** The GDD hides a mineral's name, icon and price until genuine local extraction. Bolo may know her own word for it, but she refuses to say it. This becomes character: names are earned by finding.
4. **Never promise a reward.** Objectives and the tutorial grant nothing in the GDD. Bolo explains what a choice does, not what it pays out for following her.
5. **Shortfalls are balances, not disasters.** The GDD keeps the 20-second cycle fixed and cuts output instead. Bolo says "delivers less", never "slows down".
6. **Length: 20 to 40 words per line,** the same cap as the caretaker barks. First meeting and story beats can run longer.
7. **Division of labour (proposal):** Bolo explains how a system works; the caretakers mark milestones when they happen. Where both fire on one event, the caretaker line plays first.

## Sample lines

These lines test the voice; they are drafts, not final script. Tutorial numbers match the GDD balance baseline (§3, §9, §12, §13, §17).

**First meeting**

> &#91;click\] Louder than you sounded from below. I have listened to your ship for twenty-five years. You are the one they chose. Fa-ci-li-ta-tor. ...Better than "ruler". I'm Bolo. My ancestors built under this ground, and I am the last of them. You will build on top of it. Carefully, I hope.

**Tutorial beats** (in the GDD's first-session order)

| Trigger | Line |
| --- | --- |
| Placing the first City | Choose where your first City stands. Look before you click: mountains, coasts, rivers, they change what a place is good at. Everything that matters is on the map. Nothing is hidden under the paint. |
| First City complete, Capital added | Your City came with a Capital District already inside it. Automatic, like most things your caretakers design. Everything after this, you choose. Not me. Not them. |
| Top resource bar | Top of the screen. Pop-u-la-tion: rooms against work, used over total. Energy: supply against demand. Neither piles up like money does. They're balances. When one tips, you'll hear about it. From me, probably. |
| Role buttons, first district | Five kinds of district: Energy, Production, Science, Residential, Commercial. The first of each costs three hundred Cre-dits. ...Your word for keeping count of who owes whom. Build whichever matches what you want this place to become. |
| Research screen, 450 Science | You start with four hundred and fifty Science. There is no correct first purchase. Anyone who tells you otherwise is guessing. Read the branches, pick what fits your plan, and don't let a list decide for you. |
| Before first extraction | A Production district digs whatever this ground is willing to give. I won't tell you its name. You'll learn it properly, by finding it. That's the only way a name should be learned. |
| First Ferrite extracted | Fer-rite. That is what you're calling it? ...Fine. My people had their own word. You'll hear it when I trust you— When it's relevant. |
| Processing or trade | Stone alone won't build much. A Goods Factory turns four into two Consumer Goods every twenty economy seconds. Or research Trade Access and buy them. One costs stone and time, the other costs Credits. Your choice. |
| Objectives panel | Your goals sit in the panel at the side. It's a map, not a leash. Tick things off in any order you like. Fold it away if it nags. The world won't stop. |
| First energy shortfall | Demand has passed supply. Everything that needs power still runs its twenty seconds; it just delivers less. Not nothing: less. Build more supply, or pause something hungry. A balance, not a disaster. |
| First population shortfall | Every room is taken and the work still wants hands. Output drops across the whole colony, not just here. More housing fixes it, wherever you put it. Rooms are rooms. |

## Open questions and canon flags

- [ ] **Timeline.** If Bolo came up when I-330 landed, she was about 10, and alone underground before that. Is that right, or did she come up recently? This bible assumes she surfaced at 10 and has watched in secret for 25 years.
- [ ] **When does the player meet her?** If it is before the first City, she answers "are we alone?" in minute one. The lore notes say the primer holds lore back "until the probe". Her meeting point decides whether that rule bends.
- [ ] **Caretaker overlap.** The caretaker barks already cover the same Type 0 milestones. Confirm the split: Bolo explains, caretakers mark the moment.
- [ ] **"The Fifty".** Approve or replace the short name for the 50 immortals.
- [ ] **Undersoil words.** Approve the phonology and the three placeholder words, or supply your own.
- [ ] **Hidden minerals.** Bolo refuses to name minerals before discovery, to keep the GDD's no-leak rule. Confirm this is how you want her to handle it.
- [ ] **Where the Fifty went.** Is their planet a later destination, for example a Type 1 target? Bolo's lines about them should know as much as she does and no more.
- [ ] **Remnants' origin.** Should Bolo lean towards "native" or "from space" for now? The sample lines stay neutral.
- [ ] **Primer.** If the primer has rules for Personous, adding it to the project would let this bible check against it.
