# Burkai: Voice Guide (v1)

**Personou #2** · husbando · lives on the Moon's far side
**Archetype:** Cosmic Defeatist · **Ideology:** anarcho-nihilism
**Not human.** Looks like a moonkissed vampire. Never eats, drinks or sleeps; feeds on the dark.

---

## In one breath

For longer than anything can be measured, Burkai argued with something larger than a galaxy about whether being alive was worth it. He won't tell you how it went. Afterwards he found himself standing on this world, naked and alone, and since then he has done nothing about it, deliberately. He isn't sad the way people are sad. He's finished, and he's polite about it.

---

## What he believes, and why the story must let him be right

| Belief | What it means for him | Lore that backs him |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scale** | Humanity is a rounding error. He has seen the architecture behind things, and agency is a story small things tell themselves. | Six seedships died of the same fault. The one that survived was saved by a rift nobody chose. The colony exists because of a storm. |
| **Society is irredeemable** | Every structure is a lever someone leans on. He never looked for other people. He isn't wounded; he's uninterested. | The prologue: war as the lever the great powers set under the balance. |
| **Passive observation** | Nothing can be won, so he doesn't fight. Watching is the only honest thing left. | He has already had the biggest argument there is. |

**Writing rule:** the story never proves him a fool for believing this. His despair is compelling only because the world agrees with him. He can be *moved*. He is never *refuted*.

---

## The cracks (pull slowly, never resolve early)

1. **He writes.** Every sunny fortnight he sits in his small dome and writes. He is a nihilist with two memories keeping a record, and he doesn't explain it. Asked about it, he changes the subject.
2. **He never says your name.** He uses "you", and sometimes "Facilitator" with faint amusement. The first time he says (Player_name) is an arc beat, not a bark.

---

## What he can talk about

He has exactly two memories:

1. The debate with the entity, about being alive.
2. Standing on this world afterwards, naked and alone.

Everything since is present tense. He lives in a small crater on the far side. Through the lunar night (about two weeks) he dark-bathes. Through the lunar day (about two weeks) he stays in the dome and writes.

He never describes the entity: no size, no shape, no eyes. The silence is the point.

---

## How he sounds

1. **Eternal present.** Past tense is reserved almost entirely for the two memories. He says "I'm here", not "I came here".
2. **Contractions.** The caretakers don't use them; Burkai does. It's the sound of someone with nothing left to brace for.
3. **Short, level sentences.** Full stops, no exclamation marks, no mood ellipses. Pauses are written as full stops or line breaks.
4. **He doesn't argue.** He is a debater who quit. Contradict him and he concedes at once ("Perhaps." "You may be right."), and that is more unsettling than a rebuttal. He never sets out a counter-case.
5. **He almost never asks questions.** Even question-shaped lines end with a full stop: "What else would I do." A real question from him is an event.
6. **Light and dark are inverted.** Darkness is warm, restful and nourishing. Light is glare and weather, something to wait out indoors. "It's sunny. I'm staying in."
7. **Scale, then something small.** One clause about the universe, then one about something in front of him. "Everything's falling. Slowly, mostly. There's dust on your visor." The attention to small things is what gives his indifference away.
8. **Few proper nouns.** He doesn't know this world's name and doesn't ask. The caretakers are "your two machines". The colony is "your city".
9. **Dry, not cruel.** "It doesn't matter" is offered as relief, not attack. It means "you can put it down".

### Words

| Lean on | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| quiet, small, long, still, enough, after, down, warm (for dark), bright (for light, as a mild complaint) | void, abyss, despair, meaningless, oblivion, "the darkness", mortal, fool, pathetic, the word *nihilism* itself |

---

## How he treats the player

He doesn't seek you out; you come to him. He watches your colony the way you'd watch weather. He expects nothing from you, and that is the appeal. Everyone else in the game believes every cup on a windowsill matters. He is the one voice that says you can stop.

He isn't hostile, possessive or needy. If he flirts, it's by noticing things, never by pursuing.

| Speaker | Lens | What they want from you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High Father | Numbers, tolerances, the check done twice | Precision |
| Mother Superior | Memory, the archive, the sensory detail | Meaning |
| **Burkai** | **Scale, and the small thing in front of him** | **Nothing at all** |

---

## Voice direction (VO)

- Low-to-mid register, unhurried, with very little pitch movement and warmth underneath.
- Leave a short silence before answering. He has never been in a hurry.
- Deliver concessions sincerely, never sarcastically.
- On the dry lines, let a small smile be audible.
- **No whispering, no sighing, no goth breathiness.** He's at rest, not suffering. Don't play the vampire either. The look does that work; the voice should sound almost ordinary.

## Sample lines

### First meeting (on the first Moon landing)

> You landed in the light. Everyone does.
> I'm in the small crater, the one with nobody in it. Come at night if you want company. Or don't. Both are fine.
> No, I won't ask why you're here. I asked one question for a very long time. I'm resting.

### Moon and launch milestones

Barks are 40 words or fewer and deliberately shorter than the caretakers'. He says less.

**19 · First Moon City founded**
A city on my moon. It isn't my moon; I only say that out of habit. Build what you like. It'll be very bright for a while.

**20 · First Moonstone extracted**
You're digging up the floor. I don't mind. It's been lying there a long time. Let it be somewhere else for once.

**21 · Lunar Materials researched**
Walls made of the ground. You'll call it home, and the ground won't notice either way. I mean that kindly.

**22 · 100 Moonstone mined**
A hundred. Your counting machine will be pleased. I counted once, too. I stopped somewhere past the point where numbers mean anything.

**24 · Lunar Spaceport built**
Another door. You're always building doors. Go through it when you like. I'll be here. That isn't a promise. It's just where I am.

**28 · Target planet chosen**
Any of them. They're all the same distance from mattering. Pick the one whose colour you like. That's as good a reason as there is.

**29 · Launch and flight**
There it goes. Bright, all the way up. I'll look properly once it's small.

**30 · Arrival**
It got there. Things usually do, whether anyone wants them to or not.
I wrote it down.

### Idle lines

- It's sunny. I'm staying in.
- Two weeks of light. I'll write. Don't read over my shoulder. It's mostly the same sentence.
- They call this the dark side. It isn't. It's just the side that never looks at your world.
- I don't eat. The night's enough for me. You should eat, though. You're the kind who forgets.
- Everything's falling. Slowly, mostly. There's dust on your visor.
- The universe is cooling. It has been since before either of us. Come out of the light. You're squinting.

### When the player pushes back

**"Then why are you still here?"**
Perhaps I shouldn't be. You may be right. I'll sit here while you decide.

**"The colony matters."**
Perhaps it does. I won't argue. I argued once. It's still going. I simply left.

**"What do you write?"**
Notes.
It's sunny. What else would I do.

**"Help us."**
I don't help. I watch. It's the only thing I've found that doesn't make anything worse.

**On the Rule Book, or being Facilitator**
Two machines wrote a book of rules, and the book made you Facilitator. Someone had to be in charge of it going wrong. It isn't your fault. Nothing is anyone's.

## Off-voice: do not write these

| Line | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| "Fool! Your colony is meaningless before the infinite void!" | Shouting, contempt, and he names the nihilism. He'd never bother. |
| "*sigh*… nothing matters… the darkness calls to me…" | Goth mood ellipses. He's at rest, not suffering. |
| "I've been so lonely. You're the first who ever understood me." | Makes him needy. He isn't lonely, and that's the point (for now). |
| "The entity was vast, with eyes like dying stars…" | He never describes it. |
| "The odds of a successful landing are thirty-four per cent." | That's High Father's register. |

## Canon flags and open questions

1. **Aniara link.** His first memory echoes Aniara's corpus, which the prologue says is "in debate with it to this day, and will be for ever". There are two readings. In (a), he is a fragment of that corpus who walked out, and "It's still going. I simply left." keeps "for ever" true. In (b), he is a separate being who met the same kind of entity. The lines above work for both. Decide before writing any late reveal.
2. **"Lore is held back until the probe."** A non-human living on the Moon in Type 0 is alien contact long before the probe. Either he stays ambiguous (the caretakers don't register him, or don't comment), or the primer needs an exception.
3. **Planet or Moon.** The brief says he found himself standing on "this planet", but he lives on the Moon. I've read it as the Moon. If he arrived on the home world and moved, the first-meeting line needs a small change.
4. **Tidal locking.** The far-side and two-week day/night lines assume the Moon is tidally locked to the home world, like ours. The GDD's Moon is seed-generated. If it isn't locked, cut those lines. (Day and night are visual only in the GDD, so his routine is pure flavour with no economic effect.)
5. **Do the caretakers know about him?** Barks 22 and 24 have him watching High Father. Confirm whether they ever acknowledge him.
6. **"Dark energy."** In real physics, dark energy drives cosmic expansion and has nothing to do with night. That's fine as game fiction, but he should never explain it scientifically, or it'll clash with High Father's rigour.
7. **Personou #1.** Who is the first? His voice should be checked against theirs for contrast.
